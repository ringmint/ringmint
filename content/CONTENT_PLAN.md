## Positioning and business objective

**Custom rings built around the person and the stone, with family colored-gemstone expertise and global trade relationships dating to the 1970s.** Ring Mint works with unusual colored gemstones and both colored and colorless natural and lab-grown diamonds. Colored stones are a particular differentiator; diamonds remain a core business, with no assumption that one origin is best for every client.

Use the family's history accurately: the family's relationships date to the 1970s; Ring Mint itself is a newer business. Do not imply Ring Mint was founded in the 1970s, turn experience into an unsupported rarity/exclusivity claim, or inflate qualifications. Verify precise dates and credentials against the About page and Chloe's records.

The objective is **qualified custom-jewelry inquiries, consultations and commissioned work**, not simply article count or general jewelry traffic. Readers should understand whether we can solve their particular sourcing/design problem and how to begin.

### What the customer discovery example actually taught us

The shared ChatGPT conversation recommended Ring Mint for tourmaline sourcing, from-scratch CAD, revisions and a wax try-on. ## Evidence and voice standards

Chloe's voice is personal, specific and candid: what you would actually say while helping someone choose a stone. Avoid invented bench anecdotes, generic luxury language, universal savings promises and self-awarded “best” claims.

For each brief collect: **one real question, one first-hand observation, one original visual or artifact, and one meaningful tradeoff**. A voice note is enough to start. Never fabricate a test, customer, sample size, supplier relationship, image, quote or price. Old inventory titles such as “We bought 20” or “We tested 8” are research proposals until the work actually exists; rewrite or hold them if not conducted.

| Topic type | Required material before review |
|---|---|
| Colored gemstone / fancy diamond | Actual examples where possible; identity, origin/treatment disclosures as applicable; consistent-light photos/video; relevant laboratory or gemological sources; care limitations |
| Diamond selection / comparison | Disclosed natural or lab origin, real report/examples, visual comparison beyond grades, dated sources for market claims |
| Process / style | Original CAD, wax or finished detail; dimensions/constraints where useful; Chloe's actual process rather than an assumed one |
| Pricing | Dated public retail comparables with specs and limits; actual Ring Mint project prices only when explicitly approved; never publish wholesale/invoices by default |
| Investigation / data study | Documented method, date, sample and limitations; preserve evidence; no unsupported accusations |
| Customer story | Approved factual brief, photos, consent for quotes/identifiers; what the client wanted, decisions, result, relevant link and CTA |

Do not imply synthetic versions of every gemstone are commercially available. The “lab-grown tourmaline” article must verify materials carefully. Laboratory identity, geographic-origin opinions, chain of custody and ethical claims are different things. Use authoritative sources when discussing durability/treatment and explain suitability for the person's wear habits.

For overlapping topics, check the actual existing page first. Expand the owner page or narrow the angle rather than splitting one intent into many thin articles. Existing wax, timeline, custom-vs-customizable, $5,000, toi-et-moi, lab-vs-moissanite and IGI/GIA posts should be linked and refreshed as needed, not recreated.
