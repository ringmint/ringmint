            <p>Engagement season is real and it is short. The stretch from Thanksgiving to Valentine's Day carries a large share of the year's proposals, and nearly nine in ten proposers <a href="https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study" class="text-link" rel="noopener">pop the question with a ring already in hand</a>, according to The Knot's 2026 study of 10,474 couples. Which means the ring has to exist before the moment does.</p>
            <p>A custom ring takes <a href="/blog/how-long-custom-engagement-ring-takes/" class="text-link">three to six weeks</a>. Below are the dates that follow from that, occasion by occasion.</p>

            <h2>The order-by dates</h2>
            <p>Three columns, because "the deadline" depends on what you are willing to give up. <strong>Comfortable</strong> gives you several design rounds, a mailed wax model, and a buffer. <strong>Standard</strong> works well if you are decisive and skip the model. <strong>Last call</strong> means an available stone, one or two design rounds, and no room for anything going wrong.</p>

            <p>Two of those cells deserve a warning label, and they are covered below.</p>

            <h2>Why do the December dates need a buffer?</h2>
            <p><strong>Because everyone else wants a ring at the same time, and the people who actually make rings are a small group.</strong> From mid-November, casting houses, setters and hand engravers all run longer queues. A setting that turns around in three days in July can take eight in early December.</p>
            <p>This is the part that catches people out. The design work is unaffected, so the process feels like it is moving normally, and then the physical making takes half again as long as it would have in the autumn. Every date in the table already includes a modest allowance for this. If you are starting inside two weeks of a last-call date, say so at the outset so the schedule can be built honestly rather than optimistically.</p>
            <p>Shipping is the second December problem. Insured overnight carriers get slower and less reliable in the last two weeks of the month, and a ring sitting in a depot on December 23 is a genuinely bad evening. Ring Mint ships fully insured and builds transit time into the schedule, but the sane move is to have the ring in your hands several days before you need it, not the morning of.</p>

            <h2>The New Year's Eve trap</h2>
            <p>Look at the New Year's Eve row again. The standard start date is <strong>November 26, which is Thanksgiving itself</strong>, and the last call is December 10, right in the middle of the busiest fortnight of the trade's year.</p>
            <p>New Year's Eve looks like it buys you an extra week over Christmas. In practice it does not, because the extra week is entirely consumed by the holiday slowdown, and because the workshops that would make your ring are closed for part of it. Treat a New Year's Eve proposal as though it were a Christmas one and start on the Christmas dates.</p>

            <h2>The Valentine's Day trap</h2>
            <p>The comfortable start date for Valentine's Day is December 27, which is a date on which essentially nobody in the jewelry trade is working, and on which you are probably not thinking about ring design either.</p>
            <p>The practical version: either begin the conversation before Christmas, in the first half of December, and accept that the design phase will pause over the holidays, or plan to start properly in the first week of January and use the standard column rather than the comfortable one. Both work. What does not work is deciding on January 20 that Valentine's Day would be nice.</p>

            <h2>What if you have already missed the date?</h2>
            <p><strong>Then do not rush a custom build, because a rushed custom ring is the one thing worse than a delayed one.</strong> There are two decent options and one bad one.</p>
            <ul>
              <li><strong>Propose with a placeholder and design the real ring together afterwards.</strong> This is more common than people think, it removes every deadline from the process, and it means the person wearing the ring for the next fifty years had a say in it.</li>
              <li><strong>Buy something ready-made now and make the custom piece later</strong>, as an upgrade or as a wedding band set. Ring Mint sources ready-made pieces as well as making them.</li>
              <li><strong>Compress the custom build into ten days.</strong> This is the bad one. Something will be compromised, usually the stone, because the only stones available at no notice are the ones nobody else wanted.</li>
            </ul>
            <p>The date is not the point. Nobody remembers, five years later, that the proposal happened in February rather than December. They do remember the ring.</p>

            <h2>How to start, briefly</h2>
            <p>You need less than you think to begin. A rough budget and the date are genuinely enough; the stone type, shape and style can all be worked out afterwards, and working them out is the first part of the job rather than a prerequisite for it. If you have a screenshot of something you like, bring it. If you do not, that is also fine, and describing what the person is like is often more useful than a reference image anyway.</p>
            <p>What does help enormously is knowing the ring size, or having a ring of theirs you can borrow. If neither is possible, a <a href="/blog/wax-model-ring-try-on/" class="text-link">wax model</a> can settle it, though that is the week you will not have if you are near a last-call date.</p>

            <p class="aside">One thing from the bench: the busiest week of my year is the first week of December, and it is almost entirely people who assumed a ring could be made in ten days. It sometimes can. It is never the ring they would have had if they had called in October.</p>

Q: When should I order an engagement ring for a Christmas proposal?
A: Start a custom ring by November 6, 2026 to be comfortable, which leaves room for several design rounds and a mailed wax model. November 20 still works if you are decisive about the stone. December 4 is the last call, and it requires a stone that is already available and quick decisions on the design. The trade runs its longest queues from mid-November, so earlier is materially better.
Q: Is New Year's Eve a safer deadline than Christmas for a custom ring?
A: No. New Year's Eve appears to buy an extra week over Christmas Day, but the holiday slowdown and workshop closures consume all of it. Treat a New Year's Eve proposal as though it were a Christmas one and use the Christmas dates: start by November 12 to be comfortable and December 10 at the very latest.
Q: When should I start a custom ring for Valentine's Day 2027?
A: Either begin in the first half of December 2026 and accept that the design phase will pause over the holidays, or start properly in the first week of January 2027. The nominal comfortable date of December 27 falls when almost nobody in the trade is working. January 24 is the last call for February 14.
Q: Why do engagement rings take longer to make in December?
A: Because casting houses, stone setters and hand engravers are all working through the year's heaviest demand at the same time. A setting that turns around in three days in July can take eight in early December. Insured shipping also slows in the last two weeks of the month, so the ring should be in your hands several days before you need it.
Q: What should I do if I have missed the deadline for a holiday proposal?
A: Propose with a placeholder ring and design the real one together afterwards, or buy a ready-made piece now and commission the custom ring later. Both are better than compressing a custom build into ten days, because at that notice the only stones available are the ones nobody else selected, and the stone is the part you cannot fix later.
