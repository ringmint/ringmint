            <h2>Who is who?</h2>
            <p><strong>Ring Concierge</strong> was founded in 2013 by Nicole Wegman and is based in New York. Their <a href="https://ringconcierge.com/pages/rc-about" class="text-link" rel="noopener">about page</a> lists fine jewelry stores in Soho and on Madison Avenue in New York, at Westfield Century City in Los Angeles, and at Town Center in Boca Raton, plus a bridal showroom in Midtown Manhattan by appointment. They sell ready-to-wear fine jewelry, including a signature tennis collection, alongside engagement rings and wedding bands, and they offer an online ring builder.</p>
            <p><strong>Ring Mint</strong> was founded in 2025 by Chloe Alpert, a second-generation jeweler who has worked in the trade since 2009 and grew up in her mother's business, Teaman Jewelry, founded in 1979. There is no store, no showroom, no employees and no held inventory. Every piece is designed from scratch and made to order, with stones sourced only after a client describes what they want. The <a href="/about/" class="text-link">founder background is here</a> and <a href="/reviews/" class="text-link">every client review is here</a>.</p>
            <p>The names are a coincidence. "Ring concierge" is also a generic description of a service, which is part of why the collision happens at all.</p>

            <h2>When Ring Concierge is the better choice</h2>
            <p>Genuinely, and this is not a courtesy paragraph:</p>
            <ul>
              <li><strong>You want to try rings on in a store.</strong> Nothing replaces putting a finished ring on a hand under real light, and if you are in New York, Los Angeles or Boca Raton you can do exactly that.</li>
              <li><strong>You want to buy something today.</strong> A brand with inventory can hand you a finished piece. A made-to-order business structurally cannot.</li>
              <li><strong>You want the brand.</strong> That is a real thing to want. A recognised name has value to some people and pretending otherwise would be silly.</li>
              <li><strong>You are shopping for ready-to-wear fine jewelry</strong> rather than a commissioned piece.</li>
              <li><strong>You were looking for their careers page or customer service.</strong> Ring Mint cannot help with either. Go to <a href="https://ringconcierge.com" class="text-link" rel="noopener">ringconcierge.com</a> directly.</li>
            </ul>

            <h2>When a business like Ring Mint is the better choice</h2>
            <ul>
              <li><strong>The ring you want is not in anyone's catalog.</strong> An heirloom stone in a non-standard size, a band that has to sit flush against an existing ring, an asymmetric setting. These are the cases where <a href="/blog/custom-vs-customizable-ring/" class="text-link">a ring builder runs out of road</a>.</li>
              <li><strong>You want the overhead out of the price.</strong> A store pays for a lease, a sales floor and every piece in the case that did not sell, and all of it is recovered in what does sell. Ring Mint clients typically pay 30 to 50 percent less than the retail price of a comparable ring for that reason alone.</li>
              <li><strong>You would rather text than book an appointment.</strong> Most of the process here happens over WhatsApp.</li>
              <li><strong>You want one person accountable end to end</strong>, from the first question to the insured delivery, rather than a handoff between departments.</li>
            </ul>

            <h2>Where Ring Mint is genuinely weaker</h2>
            <p>A comparison written by one of the parties is worth nothing without this section.</p>
            <ul>
              <li><strong>You cannot walk in.</strong> There is no showroom, anywhere, and there is not going to be one. A <a href="/blog/wax-model-ring-try-on/" class="text-link">wax model mailed to your door</a> is the substitute, and it is a good one, but it is not the same as a tray of finished rings.</li>
              <li><strong>Nothing is available immediately.</strong> Everything is made to order, so the floor is about <a href="/blog/how-long-custom-engagement-ring-takes/" class="text-link">three weeks</a>. If you need a ring next Tuesday, Ring Mint is the wrong call and will say so.</li>
              <li><strong>It is one person.</strong> That is the appeal for many clients and a real constraint for others. Capacity is finite, and August is not January.</li>
              <li><strong>The track record is shorter.</strong> Ring Concierge has been operating since 2013. Ring Mint launched in 2025, on the back of a family business running since 1979, which is not the same thing as a decade of its own trading history. The <a href="/reviews/" class="text-link">reviews page</a> shows everything there is, unedited.</li>
            </ul>

            <h2>How to choose, in one question</h2>
            <p><strong>Does the ring you want already exist?</strong></p>
            <p>If yes, or close enough that a small adjustment would do it, buy it from someone who has it. You will get it faster and you will get to try it on first. That is a good outcome and there is no reason to overcomplicate it.</p>
            <p>If no, and you keep finding rings that are almost right, then no amount of browsing will fix that, and the answer is to have one drawn. That is the whole reason a business like Ring Mint exists.</p>

            <p class="aside">One thing from the bench: I have told people to go buy a ring somewhere else more than once, usually because their deadline was too close or because they had already found something they loved. It costs a sale and it is obviously the right call. A ring nobody is happy with is worse for everybody than a sale I did not make.</p>
