Three custom lab-grown diamond necklaces for wedding gifts, around $900 each

By Chloe Alpert · September 12, 2026 · 4 min read

A client needed three diamond necklaces as wedding gifts for the mothers and a sister. They had been seeing retail options between $2,000 and $3,500 per necklace.

We started with CAD, sourced the diamonds, and finished all three necklaces in our Pennsylvania studio. Each features an approximately 6mm round lab-grown diamond, D or E color, VVS2 clarity, and an individual IGI grading report.

Two necklaces are in 14k yellow gold. One is in 14k white gold. The client’s price: around $900 each, or approximately $2,700 for all three.

Three coordinated gifts, with a choice of gold

The brief was a set of gifts for three people close to the wedding. A diamond solitaire necklace gives each recipient the same simple design, while the choice of yellow or white gold makes room for individual preferences.

We kept the focus on a single round diamond in a four-prong setting. Two pieces use the warmth of 14k yellow gold; the third pairs the diamond with 14k white gold.

Then, sourcing the lab-grown diamonds

For this commission, we sourced three approximately 6mm round lab-grown diamonds. Their color grades are D or E, and all three have VVS2 clarity and individual IGI reports.

Those specifications describe the stones selected for this order. A photograph can show the appearance of a necklace, but it cannot establish a diamond’s color grade, clarity, or identity.

We describe the size in millimeters because that was part of the brief. Approximately 6mm refers to the diamond’s diameter; it is not a carat weight. Each stone’s exact weight and measurements belong on its individual report.

What the client is paying—and what replacement value means

The client’s price is around $900 per necklace, bringing the three-piece commission to approximately $2,700.

Before coming to Ring Mint, the client reported seeing retail necklaces priced between $2,000 and $3,500 each. That was their shopping context, rather than a documented comparison of identical pieces with matching specifications.

My retail replacement valuation is $2,000 per necklace. That is a separate figure from the client’s purchase price. It is not a resale offer or a promise of what the necklace would fetch if sold.
